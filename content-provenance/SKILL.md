# Content Provenance

Provenance asks where a media item appeared, what earlier or later versions exist, and which parts of its history can be independently supported. It supplements [image](../image-authentication/SKILL.md), [video](../video-authentication/SKILL.md), [audio](../audio-authentication/SKILL.md), and [synthetic-media](../synthetic-media-analysis/SKILL.md) authentication. A first indexed occurrence is not necessarily the first publication or capture; a valid credential is not proof that the depicted event happened.

Use [SWGDE *Best Practices for Image Authentication*, 18-I-001-2.0](https://www.swgde.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025-03-03-Best-Practices-for-Image-Authentication-18-I-001-2.0.pdf) and [*Best Practices for Digital Video Authentication*, 23-V-001-1.2](https://www.swgde.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/23-V-001-1.2-Best-Practices-for-Digital-Video-Authentication.pdf) for source and processing-history questions. [SWGDE *Guidelines for Video Evidence Canvassing and Collection*, 20-V-002-1.0](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/20-v-002-swgde-guidelines-for-video-evidence-canvassing-and-collection/) informs online collection and source logging. Apply local legal authority and laboratory policy to collection.

## Suggested workflow

1. **Frame the proposition and preserve.** State whether the question is earliest *known* occurrence, likely source file, lineage between two files, capture device, publisher identity, or AI-tool involvement. Record rival explanations: repost, platform transcode, crop, screenshot, syndication, metadata rewrite, or deliberate impersonation. Hash the supplied bytes and a verified working copy; preserve the original filename, acquisition path, receipt time, and custody. See [evidence integrity](../multimedia-authentication/references/evidence-integrity.md).
2. **Build local fingerprints.** Record SHA-256 for exact identity. For image/video, extract selected frames at documented source timestamps, orientation, crop, and color settings; compute perceptual hashes or visual descriptors for candidate retrieval. For audio, compute a documented acoustic fingerprint or landmark features. Similarity scores locate candidates only; confirm a match by side-by-side content, distinctive details, geometric/temporal alignment, and known transformations. Avoid treating visually similar stock imagery or repeated scenes as one file lineage.
3. **Reverse lookup systematically.** Search exact file/hash or URL where a corpus supports it. For images, search the whole image and distinctive crops with at least two independent indexes where useful; search visible text by OCR, quoted phrases, logos, and source filenames. For video, search representative keyframes from opening, middle, closing, and disputed intervals; search title/caption/transcript phrases and platform identifiers. For audio, search distinctive speech/music phrases, transcript excerpts, or authorized fingerprint indexes. Record every query, crop/frame hash, search engine and settings, date/time with timezone, result URL, account, snippet, and access conditions. Search results are incomplete and ranking is not chronology.
4. **Acquire candidate occurrences.** Preserve the actual page and media bytes when authorized, plus URL, account/handle, platform ID, publication and modification claims, retrieval time, headers or API response when available, and a page capture. Prefer direct media download to a screen recording; label platform transcodes. Record redirects, deleted/unavailable results, and whether an archive snapshot is independent or copied from the same publisher. Do not upload nonpublic evidence to public search services or watermark portals without case authority and a documented disclosure decision. Public pages can disappear; preserve leads promptly under applicable rules.
5. **Compare lineage.** For each candidate, compare exact hashes, dimensions, crop boundaries, overlays, compression generations, soundtrack, frame order, and unique defects. A higher-resolution or longer-duration version may be upstream, but can also be an upscale or composite. Build a directed candidate graph: item IDs as nodes, observed transformations as edges, and evidence for each edge. Distinguish a confirmed exact copy from a probable derivative and a mere visual/content match. A timestamp or archive capture proves only what that system recorded at that time, subject to its clock and indexing behavior.
6. **Examine metadata and provenance claims.** Compare embedded EXIF/XMP/IPTC/QuickTime/ID3/BWF fields; software, encoder, device, serial, original filename, edit history, GPS, capture/export/modify times, timezones, thumbnails, ICC/color data, stream IDs, and sidecars. Separate filesystem and platform times from embedded claims. Check internal consistency and agreement with known device/export exemplars. Metadata can be altered or stripped without changing pixels or samples; matching tags from one exporter are not independent corroboration. See [metadata and containers](../multimedia-authentication/references/metadata-and-container-analysis.md).
7. **Verify C2PA if present.** Use a current verifier on the preserved file. Record manifest availability, active manifest, claim/signature result, content binding, signer certificate and trust-chain status, timestamp/revocation information when available, actions, `digitalSourceType`, ingredients, and any missing/broken links. Inspect each ingredient separately; do not assume a signed assertion verifies its factual truth or a full unrecorded pre-credential history. Check whether later transformations stripped a manifest or whether a soft binding locates one. An absent credential is non-diagnostic. Follow the [C2PA specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/) and its [explanation of trust limits](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/explainer/Explainer.html).
8. **Check producer-specific watermark claims.** Use an official verifier for the claimed watermark family and media type, documenting service/version, exact submitted bytes or derivative, detected regions or times, full output, and known error behavior. As of October 2026, [Google's SynthID Detector](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/synth-id-ai-content/) offers checks for image, video, and audio from participating AI systems; availability and covered producers can change. A positive result supports the narrower claim that a supported watermark was detected in the tested material, not who made it, when, or whether every part of the file is generated. A negative result does not establish camera origin or exclude AI: the content may come from an unsupported system, have no mark, or have undergone destructive processing. Treat unverified third-party “SynthID detectors” as screening tools, not authoritative confirmation.
9. **Synthesize and review.** Produce a chronology with source of each date, a candidate lineage graph, and a finding for every material provenance claim: observation, comparison method, alternative, contrary evidence, and uncertainty. Distinguish *earliest located*, *earliest independently documented*, and *original capture*. Refer manipulation or synthetic-origin questions to the relevant authentication skill. Submit a court-facing opinion for independent qualified review and mark it preliminary until reviewed and authorized.

## Suggested tools and limits

| Purpose | Tool or algorithm | Evidential limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact/local similarity | SHA-256; [ImageHash](https://github.com/JohannesBuchner/imagehash) pHash/dHash; OpenCV feature matching | Perceptual hashes survive some edits but collide on similar scenes and may fail after crops or overlays. Preserve parameters and manually verify candidates. |
| Video/audio retrieval | [FFmpeg](https://ffmpeg.org/documentation.html) documented frame extraction; [Chromaprint](https://github.com/acoustid/chromaprint) for suitable audio | Frame selection and fingerprints are retrieval aids. Speech/noisy short clips may be poor matches; external indexes have coverage limits. |
| Public reverse lookup | [Google Lens](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/1325808), [TinEye](https://blog.tineye.com/how-to-use-tineye-search/), platform search, text/OCR search | These are external services, not forensic validators. Upload only with authorization; save query and result evidence. |
| Embedded claims | [ExifTool](https://exiftool.org/), [MediaInfo](https://mediaarea.net/MediaInfo), [FFprobe](https://ffmpeg.org/ffprobe.html) | Cross-check contested fields and distinguish embedded from computed values. |
| Signed provenance | [c2patool](https://github.com/contentauth/c2patool) and a second C2PA-compatible verifier when material | Record exact validator and trust-list version; cryptographic validity does not establish scene truth. |
| AI watermark | Official [SynthID Detector](https://deepmind.google/models/synthid/) or producer-specific verifier | Coverage, availability, transformations, and provider disclosure terms constrain the inference. |

The handoff is an evidence-backed provenance timeline and candidate lineage, with preserved search records and a [finding record](../multimedia-authentication/schemas/finding-schema.json). Do not elevate a search snippet, “first seen” date, unsigned metadata field, credential badge, or watermark score into a source-attribution conclusion without testing its alternatives.
